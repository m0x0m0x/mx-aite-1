# E1 — Empirical Case Studies

Scope: **named case evidence only.** Each row states what actually happened, the metric that separates it from speculation, and — for failures — the signal that was *observable before the loss*. Scoring signals derived from these cases are at the end; ref IDs resolve in `E1_SOURCES.md`.

A note on how numbers are handled: figures below are quoted from DeFiLlama / L2BEAT / CertiK / a court's filing / an on-chain trace report as of the access date (2026-10-06), except where the cell is explicitly marked. Anything I could not tie to such a source is written `UNSOURCED` rather than estimated.

## E1.3 Cases the tool must not get wrong

| case | why the surface signals mislead | what the tool should and should not conclude |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **MakerDAO / Sky after March 2020 ("Black Thursday")** | A flagship, audited, 2-year-old protocol left **$4.5M of unbacked DAI** and over **$8M of ETH was sold for free** in zero-bid auctions during a 43% one-day ETH drop (E1-D17). A naive "protocol insolvency" flag fires. | **Should:** conclude that *liquidation machinery failed under oracle/gas stress*, not that the protocol was a fraud — and that the loss was **socialised through MKR dilution via debt auction**, a public, token-funded recovery. **Should not:** conclude "fraud" or "insolvent"; the same protocol now books $204.6M of annualised revenue (E1-C18, measured), i.e. it is one of the most profitable protocols in DeFi. A flag on "unbacked debt" is correct only if paired with *who bore the loss and was it disclosed*. |
| **USDC / Circle** | Its product is indistinguishable from an algorithmic or partially-collateralised stablecoin from on-chain data alone — the reserve is entirely off-chain — and USDC **has** broken peg materially: **$0.88 on 11 Mar 2023** after SVB exposure was revealed (E1-D18, Reuters), and repeated flash prints to **$0.74** on Binance on 3 Jan 2024 that recovered within minutes (E1-D20). | **Uniswap** | By Dec 2025 the token had no fee entitlement for seven years, and volume is trivially gamed (wash trading is endemic in DEX volume; DeFiLlama itself excludes pools whose trades come from too few distinct addresses, E1-C3). A "no value capture" and "volume is fake" flags both look justified. | **Should:** conclude that usage is real *because* it is priced and LPs absorb the fee, and that value capture **now exists in the contract** (17% to buyback-and-burn from 28 Dec 2025, E1-C1, E1-C2). **Should not:** conclude "speculative" on a zero-fee-switch basis — the flag must be timestamped. **Must not** treat headline DEX volume as proof of anything without the distinct-address filter. |

| **SushiSwap today** | Cumulative volume of $251.6B (E1-C22) looks like deep, surviving utility — the same shape of number as Uniswap's $3.95T. | **Should:** note that cumulative volume is *backward-looking and dominated by incentive farming*; the honest current numbers are $31.2M 30d volume, $41.75M TVL, $6.5K 30d revenue (E1-C22). **Should not** conclude "died" — it still clears ~$2M/yr in fees and is a functioning top-20 AMM. The lesson: **never score on cumulative volume without a time-windowed ratio.** |
| **Axie Infinity** | Historic scale is enormous (a 2.7M peak daily-user figure circulates widely but **I could not source it to a primary or measurement provider, so treat it as UNSOURCED**), a sidechain was built for it, and Ronin is still a functioning chain that migrated to OP Stack in May 2026, cutting RON inflation from >20% to <1% (E1-D19). | **Should:** conclude that *current* on-chain activity is orders of magnitude below any historical claim, and that what activity exists is dominated by one-off distribution events rather than play. **Must not** infer that a large historical DAU peak is current utility, and **must not** treat the Ronin hack as proof the token was illegitimate — Sky Mavis raised $150M and reimbursed users (E1-B4). |

Every signal below was extracted from a named case above and is stated so it can be checked mechanically against chain data, a published document, or a contract. "Failure mode" is how the signal misleads — several of those are the deliberate trap cases in E1.3.
